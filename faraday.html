In one paragraph

Counts where a cavity mode's electric nodal lines meet the wall and where its magnetic field curls inside, and tests the exact law between the two. The question: Does the topology of a cavity mode's magnetic field inside the cavity follow from where the electric field's nodal lines meet the wall? The headline result: 132 of 132, TM modes of 11 cavities obey Q = χ(D) + N_wall/2, from a rectangle to a disk with two holes. Control: a distractor term in the least-squares fit gets weight 0.0000; on a 15 × 15 grid the same check fails on 25 of 320 signature entries. Code: teerthsharma/faraday on GitHub.

What it is

A metal cavity with perfectly conducting walls rings at fixed patterns, its modes. In the transverse-magnetic case the whole mode is one scalar field, the electric field E=EzE = E_zE=Ez​, which is zero on the wall. The magnetic field lies in the plane of the cavity and is the gradient of EEE turned through a right angle and divided by the wavenumber kkk:

−ΔE=k2E in D,E=0 on ∂D,H=1k (∂yE,  −∂xE).-\Delta E = k^2 E \ \text{in } D, \qquad E = 0 \ \text{on } \partial D, \qquad H = \frac{1}{k}\,\big(\partial_y E,\; -\partial_x E\big).−ΔE=k2E in D,E=0 on ∂D,H=k1​(∂y​E,−∂x​E).

Where HHH vanishes it curls like a vortex. Each zero of HHH is a critical point of EEE and carries an integer winding number, +1+1+1 at a maximum or minimum of EEE and −1-1−1 at a saddle. Add up the windings of every zero inside the cavity and the result is an integer QQQ. The electric field has its own integer on the other side. The nodal lines of EEE, the curves where it changes sign, reach the wall at some number NwallN_{\text{wall}}Nwall​ of points. Faraday tests the law that ties the two integers together, with χ(D)=1−(number of holes)\chi(D) = 1 - (\text{number of holes})χ(D)=1−(number of holes) the Euler characteristic of the cavity:

Q  =  χ(D)+12Nwall.Q \;=\; \chi(D) + \tfrac{1}{2} N_{\text{wall}} .Q=χ(D)+21​Nwall​.

The count along a one-dimensional wall fixes the count over the whole two-dimensional interior. The repository’s name for this is “the wall decides the bulk”. The law holds on every one of the 132 modes the repository’s study solves, across 11 cavities from a rectangle to a disk with two holes (section 2).

faraday is a Python package. It solves the modes on a grid, measures both integers exactly, tests the law, and then builds three things on top of it: a learned linear coupling from the electric-side integers to the magnetic-side integers, which the repository calls the God Tensor; a “quantum sector” that applies the same vortex count to complex combinations of modes; and a “Higgs coupling” that adds a position-dependent photon mass to the same equation. Each of those names is larger than the computation behind it, and sections 3 and 6 say what each one computes.

One fact frames everything else. The magnetic field here is not solved for. It is derived from EEE by finite differences (faraday/em_solver.py:374-382). The law is therefore a statement about the topology of one eigenvector seen through its own gradient, and the repository’s theory note says as much (docs/DUALITY.md:21). That is what makes it exact and checkable. It also means that nothing in this package shows an independent electric field and an independent magnetic field agreeing.

faraday belongs to the same line of work as resolvent.

The law across cavities

The multiply connected cavities are the real test of the χ(D)\chi(D)χ(D) term. In a rectangle χ(D)=1\chi(D) = 1χ(D)=1 always, so a law with a constant 1 in its place fits; only cavities with holes force the term to move. The concentric annulus has χ=0\chi = 0χ=0 and its fundamental mode has Q=0Q = 0Q=0. The disk with two holes has χ=−1\chi = -1χ=−1, and its fundamental mode has Q=−1Q = -1Q=−1: a single nodal domain with a net antivortex in it, which the law requires and the other cavities never show.

Predicting the integers without solving

For a rectangle the integers follow from the mode indices (m,n)(m, n)(m,n): mnm nmn nodal domains, Nwall=2(m−1)+2(n−1)N_{\text{wall}} = 2(m-1) + 2(n-1)Nwall​=2(m−1)+2(n−1), Q=m+n−1Q = m + n - 1Q=m+n−1 (faraday/duality.py:208-236). The geometry fixes the order of the wavenumbers, so a field never has to be solved.

How the charge changes

In a rectangle the modes TM31 and TM12 cross at an aspect ratio w/h=8/3≈1.63w/h = \sqrt{8/3} \approx 1.63w/h=8/3​≈1.63, and their charges swap in one step. If the wall is tilted so the crossing is avoided, the charge changes by another route: a nodal line detaches from the wall, NwallN_{\text{wall}}Nwall​ drops by 2 and QQQ by 1. In the tilted family the pair (Q2,Q3)(Q_2, Q_3)(Q2​,Q3​) goes from (2,3)(2, 3)(2,3) to (2,2)(2, 2)(2,2) to (3,2)(3, 2)(3,2) as the width increases, and in between both modes have Q=2Q = 2Q=2, so the pair’s total charge is not conserved. I reproduced both sweeps (experiments/duality_study.py:114-128); at w/h=1.62w/h = 1.62w/h=1.62 and tilt 0.15 the two modes sit 1.74% apart in kkk with Q=2Q = 2Q=2 on both.

A coupling that fails when the law fails

For the first 16 modes of every training cavity, faraday builds two 16-number signatures. The electric one is ej=χ(D)+Nwall,j/2e_j = \chi(D) + N_{\text{wall},j}/2ej​=χ(D)+Nwall,j​/2. The magnetic one is hj=Qjh_j = Q_jhj​=Qj​. It learns a linear map TTT from eee to hhh by least squares. Because the law makes e=he = he=h on every entry, TTT turns out to be an orthogonal projector, T2=TT^2 = TT2=T, and the size of ∥T2−T∥/∥T∥\lVert T^2 - T\rVert / \lVert T\rVert∥T2−T∥/∥T∥ is a direct readout of how far the data break the law.

At 30 × 30 the fixed subspace of TTT has dimension 11, equal to the rank of the signature matrix, and the God Score (a number in [0,1][0, 1][0,1] measuring how close TeT eTe and ThT hTh land to that subspace) is 1.0000. At 15 × 15 the dimension is 7 against rank 12 and the score is 0.8891. The coupling is a diagnostic that passes when every mode’s integers are resolved and fails visibly when they are not.

The same count on complex states

The equation −ΔE=k2E-\Delta E = k^2 E−ΔE=k2E with E=0E = 0E=0 on the wall is the Schrödinger equation of a particle in a two-dimensional box. The “quantum sector” (faraday/quantum.py) takes a complex state ψ=u+iv\psi = u + ivψ=u+iv built from two neighbouring real modes, counts its phase vortices with the same winding routine, and tests three statements.

The third statement concerns loops in shape space. Carry a real mode around a closed loop of cavity shapes and it returns with its sign flipped exactly when the loop encloses a level crossing. I reproduced the repository’s six loops: the loop around the TM31/TM12 crossing returns sign −1-1−1 for both modes (smallest overlap between consecutive steps 0.990), and the four loops that miss it return +1+1+1.

How it was made

DefinitionThe objects

A mode of a planar cavity DDD is a pair (k,E)(k, E)(k,E) with −ΔE=k2E-\Delta E = k^2 E−ΔE=k2E and E=0E = 0E=0 on ∂D\partial D∂D. Its magnetic field is H=(∂yE,−∂xE)/kH = (\partial_y E, -\partial_x E)/kH=(∂y​E,−∂x​E)/k. The charge QQQ is the total winding number of HHH around its zeros in DDD. NwallN_{\text{wall}}Nwall​ is the number of sign changes of EEE along the wall, summed over the outer wall and the wall of every hole. The signatures e,h∈R16e, h \in \mathbb{R}^{16}e,h∈R16 of a cavity collect ej=χ(D)+Nwall,j/2e_j = \chi(D) + N_{\text{wall},j}/2ej​=χ(D)+Nwall,j​/2 and hj=Qjh_j = Q_jhj​=Qj​ for its first 16 modes. The coupling T∈R16×16T \in \mathbb{R}^{16\times 16}T∈R16×16 is the minimum-norm least-squares map with Tei≈hiT e_i \approx h_iTei​≈hi​ over the training cavities, and the God Tensor is the subspace {x:Tx=x}\{x : Tx = x\}{x:Tx=x}.

The eigenproblem

The solver builds the five-point Laplacian on the grid nodes inside the cavity, with every other node pinned to zero (the perfectly conducting wall), so the matrix is symmetric and negative definite (faraday/em_solver.py:146-206). It asks scipy.sparse.linalg.eigsh for the eigenvalues nearest zero by shift-invert, σ=0\sigma = 0σ=0, and sorts the modes by ascending kkk (em_solver.py:349-356). Named shapes are rectangles and circles; any other cavity, with holes or without convexity, is a boolean mask on a uniform grid (em_solver.py:286-293). The test suite checks the rectangle against the analytic wavenumbers and the circle against Bessel zeros (tests/test_physics.py:38, 57).

ei+1,j−2ei,j+ei−1,jΔx2+ei,j+1−2ei,j+ei,j−1Δy2=−k2 ei,j.\frac{e_{i+1,j} - 2e_{i,j} + e_{i-1,j}}{\Delta x^2} + \frac{e_{i,j+1} - 2e_{i,j} + e_{i,j-1}}{\Delta y^2} = -k^2\, e_{i,j}.Δx2ei+1,j​−2ei,j​+ei−1,j​​+Δy2ei,j+1​−2ei,j​+ei,j−1​​=−k2ei,j​.

The magnetic field is then formed from EEE by central differences with c=μ=1c = \mu = 1c=μ=1, so ω=k\omega = kω=k, and the constant phase factor iii in the curl relation is dropped (em_solver.py:374-382). The signed components hx and hy are kept, along with the magnitude.

Counting the integers

The vortex charge uses discrete Stokes. The angle arg⁡H\arg HargH is taken at every node, the wrapped difference across each grid edge is computed once, and each edge enters its two neighbouring cells with opposite signs. The winding of a cell is

wp=12π∑e∈∂pwrap⁡(Δearg⁡H),w_p = \frac{1}{2\pi} \sum_{e \in \partial p} \operatorname{wrap}\big(\Delta_e \arg H\big),wp​=2π1​e∈∂p∑​wrap(Δe​argH),

and because every edge is shared, the sum of wpw_pwp​ over any region telescopes to the winding along its boundary (faraday/duality.py:45-61). A test pins the case that breaks per-cell wrapping, a zero lying exactly on a grid line (tests/test_duality.py:76). χ(D)\chi(D)χ(D) is the number of connected components minus the number of holes (duality.py:64-68). NwallN_{\text{wall}}Nwall​ walks each boundary loop of interior nodes, orders them by angle about the loop’s centroid and counts sign changes of EEE (duality.py:88-106).

The repository derives the law from the Poincaré–Hopf index theorem with boundary: the field HHH is tangent to the wall, and each point where a nodal line of EEE reaches the wall is a boundary zero of index −12-\tfrac12−21​ (duality.py:13-23). Inside each nodal domain the net charge is that domain’s Euler characteristic. That is a sketch in a docstring, not a published proof. The evidence in this essay is the count: 132 of 132 modes.

The learned coupling

learn_T stacks the signatures of the training cavities into EEE and HHH and solves the least-squares problem (faraday/god_tensor.py:347-383):

T⊤=arg⁡min⁡A∥EA−H∥F=E+H,T=(E+H)⊤.T^\top = \arg\min_{A} \lVert E A - H \rVert_F = E^{+} H, \qquad T = (E^{+}H)^\top .T⊤=argAmin​∥EA−H∥F​=E+H,T=(E+H)⊤.

On the 20 training cavities at 30 × 30, EEE and HHH are equal as arrays (I checked numpy.array_equal). Then T=(E+E)⊤=E+ET = (E^{+}E)^\top = E^{+}ET=(E+E)⊤=E+E, which is the orthogonal projector onto the span of the signature rows. I compared the learned TTT with pinv(E) @ E entry by entry: the largest difference is 4.0e-15. So the learned operator is the projector onto the span of the training signatures. What the learning adds is a number: the idempotency defect, which says whether the data obeyed the law (god_tensor.py:370-374).

The fixed point is found as a Banach contraction. Plain power iteration x↦Tx/∥Tx∥x \mapsto Tx/\lVert Tx\rVertx↦Tx/∥Tx∥ converges to the eigenvector of largest ∣λ∣\lvert\lambda\rvert∣λ∣, which need not be the eigenvalue-1 vector the God Tensor is meant to be. The code iterates the shifted inverse instead,

G(x)=(T−μI)−1x∥(T−μI)−1x∥,μ=1+10−6∥T∥,G(x) = \frac{(T - \mu I)^{-1} x}{\lVert (T - \mu I)^{-1} x \rVert}, \qquad \mu = 1 + 10^{-6}\lVert T\rVert,G(x)=∥(T−μI)−1x∥(T−μI)−1x​,μ=1+10−6∥T∥,

which contracts onto the eigenspace whose eigenvalue is nearest 1 with Lipschitz factor q=∣λ∗−μ∣/∣λ2−μ∣q = \lvert\lambda^\ast - \mu\rvert / \lvert\lambda_2 - \mu\rvertq=∣λ∗−μ∣/∣λ2​−μ∣ (god_tensor.py:84-125; docs/source/theory/banach-fixed-point.rst:36-71). An eigenvalue of exactly 1 with multiplicity above one is treated as one subspace, not a tie, and convergence stops on the step or on the eigen-residual (god_tensor.py:434-448). The step is:

# faraday/god_tensor.py:144-159 @ 1fb4c48
# Computational Faraday Tensor by Teerth Sharma (https://github.com/teerthsharma)
def banach_step(
    G: Callable[[np.ndarray], np.ndarray], x: np.ndarray
) -> tuple[np.ndarray, float]:
    """One normalised step x → G(x)/||G(x)||, aligned to the sign of ``x``.

    Eigenvectors are defined up to ±1; aligning the sign keeps a negative
    shifted eigenvalue from flipping the iterate every step.

    Returns:
        (x_new, delta): next iterate and the sign-invariant step ||x_new - x||.
    """
    y = G(x)
    y = y / np.linalg.norm(y)
    if np.dot(y, x) < 0:
        y = -y
    return y, float(np.linalg.norm(y - x))

For a projector the eigenvalues are 1 and 0, so q≈10−6q \approx 10^{-6}q≈10−6. On the 20-cavity run the iteration stops at its second step with the eigen-residual 6.1e-13 and q=1.0×10−6q = 1.0\times 10^{-6}q=1.0×10−6. The iteration starts from a random vector, so the convergence is earned and not assumed (god_tensor.py:57-65), and it is cheap because a projector is the easiest operator to find a fixed subspace of.

The quantum sector and the Higgs mass

faraday/quantum.py reuses the same winding routine on (Re⁡ψ,Im⁡ψ)(\operatorname{Re}\psi, \operatorname{Im}\psi)(Reψ,Imψ) for ψ=u+iv\psi = u + ivψ=u+iv (quantum.py:51-53). The phase winding VVV of ψ\psiψ equals its winding along the wall, and a planar curve cannot wind more than half as often as either component changes sign, so ∣V∣≤12min⁡(Nwall(u),Nwall(v))\lvert V\rvert \le \tfrac12\min\big(N_{\text{wall}}(u), N_{\text{wall}}(v)\big)∣V∣≤21​min(Nwall​(u),Nwall​(v)). What was repaired on the way

The earlier version of the repository claimed more than its code did. The commit fdb06bd and the duality commits fixed it, and the changelog lists the defects: the eigensolver skipped the fundamental mode and returned modes in descending order; Betti numbers dropped the essential class, so a single blob had Betti-0 equal to 0; the fixed-point search seeded power iteration on an eigenvector, a repelling fixed point, and reported convergence at iteration 0; and the autoencoder gradient missed a ReLU mask (CHANGELOG.md:59-79). The autoencoder is no longer used for TTT, because its ReLU erased negative charges (CHANGELOG.md:11-16).

What’s new in it

The usual route is the one the README names: Maxwell’s equations, a finite-difference frequency-domain solver, and the fields to inspect. faraday keeps that solver and changes what is read from it. Instead of inspecting amplitudes or fitting features, it measures two integers per mode, one from the electric side and one from the magnetic side, and tests an exact relation between them across cavity topologies. The repository’s first approach read the fields through persistent-homology barcodes, summarised them into 50-number vectors and compressed those with autoencoders. That route is replaced: the barcode embedding is about 99.9% pixel and bar counts, its electric and magnetic vectors were identical with cosine 1, and the coupling learned from it encoded no topology (docs/DUALITY.md:81-83). The barcode code survives in the package for the nearest-neighbour predictor, with Betti numbers that now include the essential class (CHANGELOG.md:66-69).

The fixed-point search is the second change. The repository’s own theory note explains why plain power iteration is the wrong tool for an operator that need not have 1 as its dominant eigenvalue, and why PageRank can use it (the Google matrix is column-stochastic, so 1 is guaranteed dominant) (docs/source/theory/banach-fixed-point.rst:36-45, :103-109). Shifted inverse iteration is the textbook replacement, and it comes with a computed contraction factor qqq that is reported with every fit.

The third is the use of the integers as learning targets and as a check. A model that must reproduce QQQ from χ(D)\chi(D)χ(D) and NwallN_{\text{wall}}Nwall​ either recovers the coefficients 1 and 12\tfrac1221​ or it does not; discover_coupling does exactly that fit and recovers (1.0000,0.5000)(1.0000, 0.5000)(1.0000,0.5000) with zero weight on the distractor (duality.py:239-259, tests/test_duality.py:211).

What no one else built

I name the closest published work for each part, then say what faraday adds.

The wall-and-bulk count. The domain-by-domain statement, that the net charge of ∇E\nabla E∇E inside a nodal domain is its Euler characteristic, is classical: the repository derives it from the Poincaré–Hopf index theorem for vector fields on manifolds with boundary, and Pugh gives a generalised index formula (Pugh, 1968). Two recent papers count critical points of eigenfunctions with boundary data. Judge and Mondal (2021) study critical points of Laplace eigenfunctions on polygons, and the repository cites their index formula for Neumann eigenfunctions as the closest analogue. Battaglia, Pistoia and Provenzano (2024) derive an identity for Steklov eigenfunctions that relates the indices of its critical points, its restriction to the boundary and the Euler characteristic, and which in two dimensions gives an exact count of interior critical points; the repository summarises it as interior critical points counted from χ\chiχ and boundary sign changes. The difference is the boundary condition and the form of the statement. faraday states Q=χ(D)+Nwall/2Q = \chi(D) + N_{\text{wall}}/2Q=χ(D)+Nwall​/2 for Dirichlet TM cavities, with NwallN_{\text{wall}}Nwall​ the count of nodal-line endpoints on the wall, and tests it on cavities with holes. It does not prove it in a paper. The repository’s own note is to read Judge–Mondal §4 and Bérard–Helffer (2022) §2.2 in full before claiming priority (docs/DUALITY.md:103-105). I read the abstracts, not the full texts, so I claim no priority.

The geometric phase at level crossings. The sign flip of a real mode around a diabolical point is known theory (Berry and Wilkinson, 1984) and known experiment in resonators (Lauber, Weidenhammer and Dubbers, 1994). faraday’s loop test reproduces the known sign. The repository’s claim is the pairing with the integer jump of QQQ at the same crossing (docs/QUANTUM.md:86-89, :118-121), and it marks that claim as pending a literature check.

Vortices bounded by wall data. The argument principle and the holonomy around a hole are classical complex analysis (docs/QUANTUM.md:117). The bound ∣V∣≤12min⁡Nwall\lvert V\rvert \le \tfrac12 \min N_{\text{wall}}∣V∣≤21​minNwall​, which ties the phase-vortex charge to the same wall count, is the repository’s own statement, again pending a literature check.

Photon mass and trapped light. The Anderson–Higgs mechanism and the Meissner effect are textbook. Bound states in vortex cores are known for fermions (Caroli, de Gennes and Matricon, 1964) and, in the repository’s reading, for bosonic modes on superconducting strings (Witten, 1985). faraday’s version is a scalar TM mode in a frozen tanh vortex, and what it adds is the count: one trapped mode per unit-winding vortex below g=2.299g = 2.299g=2.299 (located by bisection to ±10−4\pm 10^{-4}±10−4), and the shell sequence above it.

Persistent homology. The barcode code runs the ripser library; the package does not implement persistence.

What is left that I can state without a comparison I have not made: a runnable test harness that measures the wall law and its two close relatives on any grid-mask cavity, with negative controls (an under-resolved grid, a corrupted entry, a mass gap crossed), and a learned coupling whose failure to be a projector flags a broken law. I do not claim the law itself is new.

The God Tensor is the span of the training signatures. The learned TTT equals E+EE^{+}EE+E because the two views are equal by the law, so the “fixed subspace” of the coupling is the span of the sampled cavities’ signatures: dimension 11 for 20 cavities, from 16 numbers each. The code’s docstring calls it the subspace of “physically realisable signatures” (god_tensor.py:16). It is the span of the signatures that were sampled. I found no test that a held-out cavity’s signature lies in it. What the construction certifies is that the law holds on the training data, and the fixed-point search is quick because a projector is an easy operator to find a fixed subspace of.

Electric and magnetic fields are not independent here. The magnetic field is derived from EEE by central differences, so the law is a statement about one eigenvector’s gradient. The README’s phrase “two fields that never see each other” (README.md:390-391) holds for the code paths that measure the two integers, not for the fields.

Scope. Two-dimensional scalar TM modes with a perfectly conducting wall on a staircase grid. TE modes (Neumann) and three dimensions are untested (docs/DUALITY.md:110-111). NwallN_{\text{wall}}Nwall​ orders each wall loop by angle about its centroid, which is exact for star-shaped walls, and strongly non-convex walls need a contour walk (docs/DUALITY.md:114-116). A nodal line that grazes a small hole within one grid cell is under-resolved; the two-hole disk’s third mode needs a grid spacing of at most 0.008 (docs/DUALITY.md:56-58). The law is established by numerical counts on 11 cavities at one grid each, with a resolution comparison only for the coupling.

The quantum sector is a relabelling of classical modes. The same real eigenmodes are read as billiard states and combined into ψ=u+iv\psi = u + ivψ=u+iv. A superposition of non-degenerate modes is not stationary, so its VVV is a snapshot in time, though the bound holds at every snapshot (docs/QUANTUM.md:108-110). There is no time evolution, no spin and no measurement. The repository’s notes say the chirality identity from HHH alone holds on only 118 of 132 modes, and the God Tensor does not use it (docs/QUANTUM.md:104-107); no script in the repository computes that count, and it was not re-run here.

The Higgs coupling is a mass term in a scalar equation. The condensate is prescribed, either uniform outside a region or a product of tanh⁡2\tanh^2tanh2 vortices, not a solved Ginzburg–Landau state. The gradient couplings of the full Proca equations are neglected, all results are time-harmonic, and the box has Dirichlet edges (docs/HIGGS.md:25-36). The threshold g=2.299g = 2.299g=2.299 depends on the vortex profile. The condensate-filled hole index fails near the gap edge for small holes: 2 of 3 cases hold there, because the hole’s wall is nearly Neumann (docs/HIGGS.md:83-86).

The nearest-neighbour predictor does not use the law. predict_eh_barcode returns coupling_score = gt.god_score(), one number from the training set, for every query (faraday/predict.py:121). The README’s generalisation table (README.md:287-310) reports Betti-0 agreement, which the README itself calls “trivially satisfied” (README.md:300). I did not re-run it and do not use it.

Reproduction differences. My re-run matches the repository’s numbers except in four places. Untrapped Higgs modes obey the law on 137 of 389 here against 131 in the docs. The phase-vortex study reaches its bound with V≠0V \ne 0V=0 on 15 of 66 pairs here against 16. The coarse-grid coupling has 25 of 320 violating entries and a defect of 0.65 here against 24 and 0.63. The fine-grid defect is 3.6e-15 here against 3.4e-15. My environment installed unpinned dependencies (numpy 2.4.6 and structlog 26.1.0 against the repository’s pins 2.4.4 and 25.5.0), and I did not isolate the cause. I also did not run the 50,000-epoch daemon (execution_daemon.py) or the Sphinx documentation build.
